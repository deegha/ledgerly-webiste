description: "Draft, review, and issue a tax invoice — and what changes once it's posted."

## Start a new invoice

From the Dashboard or **Sales → Invoices**, click **New invoice**. Pick a customer — if they're new, you can add them from the same screen without leaving the invoice.

## Add line items

Each line needs an item, a quantity, and a unit price. If the item is set up with a default price and tax code, both fill in automatically — you can still override either on a specific line. The line total, subtotal, tax, and grand total all recalculate live as you type.

Two dates matter here, and they're not always the same:

- **Invoice date** — when the invoice is issued.
- **Supply date** — when the goods or service were actually delivered. Per Gazette 2481/22, this has to be recorded separately even when it matches the invoice date.

## Save as draft, or issue

A draft invoice can be edited freely — change line items, the customer, either date, anything. It hasn't posted anywhere yet.

**Issuing** an invoice is the point of no return: it's assigned its permanent invoice number, and a balanced journal entry posts to Trade Receivables and the relevant income accounts. Once issued, an invoice is locked — the fields become read-only, and a banner on the invoice tells you it's posted.

## Correcting an issued invoice

You can't edit an issued invoice directly, even to fix a typo. Instead, issue a **credit note** against it — either for the full amount (to void it entirely) or a partial amount (to adjust it down). The credit note posts its own journal entry and shows up linked from the original invoice, so the correction is visible, not hidden.

## Sending and payment

Once issued, download the invoice as a PDF or send it to the customer. The invoice's status tracks automatically as payments come in — **Issued**, **Partially paid**, then **Paid** — computed from the actual customer payments recorded against it, not a manual status field anyone has to remember to update.
