## What it answers

_"Profit is one thing — but did cash come in or go out, and why?"_ A profitable business can
still run short of cash if customers haven't paid or stock has piled up. The Cash Flow
Statement explains the change in your bank and cash balance over a period.

![The Cash Flow Statement for Acme Trading (indirect method): a green "Reconciles" banner, "Net profit for the period 43,047.35", an Operating section listing working-capital movements (Trade Receivables -72,000, Customer Advances +7,500, Trade Payables +22,000, Prepayments -1,000, Inventory -5,247.35) with "Net cash from operating -5,700.00", an Investing section ("No movement this period", 0.00), and a Financing section](/guide/reports/cash-flow.jpg "Cash Flow — start from net profit, adjust for non-cash and working-capital moves, split by activity")

## How to read it (indirect method)

It **starts from net profit** and adjusts to reach the actual cash movement:

- **Operating** — net profit, plus back non-cash charges (depreciation), plus/minus changes in
  working capital. A **rise in Trade Receivables is negative** (you earned it but haven't
  collected it); a **rise in Trade Payables is positive** (you've had the benefit but not paid
  yet). Here operating cash is **-5,700.00** despite a 43,047 profit — because receivables and
  inventory grew.
- **Investing** — buying or selling fixed assets. Nothing this period.
- **Financing** — owner capital, loans, drawings, and movements in Retained Earnings.

## What it reconciles to

The green banner: _"computed net change in cash matches the actual Bank/Cash movement."_ The
three sections add up to exactly the change in your bank and cash account balances over the
period — which you can also see on the [Trial Balance](/guide/reports/trial-balance) at the
two dates.

Period start and end.
