## Forget what the words sound like

In everyday speech, "credit" sounds positive and "debit" sounds like something's being taken
away. In bookkeeping they mean neither. **Debit just means the left side of an entry. Credit
means the right side.** That's the whole definition.

Whether a debit raises or lowers an account depends entirely on _what kind of account it is_.

## The rule, in one table

| Account type                                          | A debit…     | A credit…    |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| **Asset** (cash, bank, receivables, stock, equipment) | increases it | decreases it |
| **Liability** (payables, loans, tax owed)             | decreases it | increases it |
| **Equity** (owner's capital, retained earnings)       | decreases it | increases it |
| **Income** (sales, other revenue)                     | decreases it | increases it |
| **Expense** (cost of sales, rent, wages)              | increases it | decreases it |

You don't need to memorise this to use Ledgerly — the posting rules apply it for you. But it
explains what you're looking at when you open a report.

## Seeing it on the Trial Balance

The Trial Balance lists every account with its balance in either the Debit or the Credit
column, depending on the type:

![The Trial Balance for Acme Trading: assets and expenses (Cash, Bank, Trade Receivables, Inventory, Cost of Sales, Operating Expenses) in the Debit column; liabilities, equity and income (Trade Payables, Retained Earnings, Sales Revenue) in the Credit column; totals equal at 149,000.00](/guide/bookkeeping-basics/trial-balance.jpg "Assets and expenses sit in Debit; liabilities, equity and income sit in Credit — and the two columns match")

- **Cash, Bank, Trade Receivables, Inventory** — assets — carry **debit** balances.
- **Trade Payables, Customer Advances, Retained Earnings, Sales Revenue** — liabilities,
  equity, income — carry **credit** balances.
- **Cost of Sales, Operating Expenses** — expenses — carry **debit** balances.

The **Total** row shows 149,000.00 on each side. Equal totals is what "the books balance"
looks like on this screen.

## Why it's set up this way

It's a convention chosen so the arithmetic always works out: for every entry, debits equal
credits, so across _all_ entries, total debits equal total credits. The moment they don't,
there's a mistake — and it's caught before it can distort a report.
