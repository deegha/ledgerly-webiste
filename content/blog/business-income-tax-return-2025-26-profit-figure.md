At a media briefing on 19 October, the Inland Revenue Department asked about 1.2 million registered individual taxpayers to file their 2025/26 income tax returns online by **30 November 2026**. Inland Revenue Commissioner Nandana Kumara also asked people to file early: "We can only support submissions within our system's capacity, so it is crucial not to wait until the deadline."

If you run a shop, a trading business or a small service firm as a sole proprietor or partner, there is one detail in that briefing worth knowing. The quick, pre-filled return isn't for you.

## Simple return or standard return?

The IRD's own guide to the 2025/26 individual return sets out two e-filing options: the **Standard Return** and the **Simplified Return**. Only people who have **employment and interest income alone** can use the simplified option. Anyone with business income completes the standard return, and business income goes in **Schedule 2 – Business Income**.

According to the IRD guide, Schedule 2 covers:

- income from a sole proprietorship;
- your share of business income from a partnership.

Each is declared after deducting any allowable business losses for the year. The guide also notes that profits from selling electricity generated by solar panels at your premises count as business income.

**The year in question:** the return covers income from **1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026**. The IRD Tax Calendar 2026 confirms that individual, partnership and corporate income tax returns for Y/A 2025/26 are due on **30 November 2026**.

**Filing:** e-filing is mandatory for individual taxpayers. The IRD guide notes that senior citizens may choose to file manually or online, and people with the Commissioner-General's approval can file manually. You log in to RAMIS through ird.gov.lk with a password or a one-time password. According to reports of the briefing, the portal has been open since 1 July and the IRD hotline is **1944**.

## Who has to file

According to reports of the briefing, **everyone registered** must file a 2025/26 return. That includes people with no taxable income and people whose tax was already deducted through PAYE/APIT or withholding tax on interest. The IRD's guide says that a late, missing or incorrect return can lead to penalties. If you're unsure where you stand, ask your accountant or call 1944.

**A note on TINs.** At the same briefing, according to Colombo Times, the Commissioner said every citizen aged 18 or over must obtain a TIN under a 2023 gazette. He added that having a TIN doesn't by itself mean you must register for or pay income tax. Registration is required when annual income exceeds **Rs. 1.8 million**. Separately, an IRD notice says that from **1 November 2026** you'll need a valid TIN certificate, or the IRD's e-Services verification printout, to open a bank account or register a business.

## What "a profit figure" actually takes

The standard return asks for your business income for the year, after allowable losses. It also comes with a **Statement of Assets and Liabilities** as at **31 March 2026**. For property held as part of a business, the IRD guide asks for the balance of your capital and current accounts at that date, with **a copy of the balance sheet attached where applicable**.

So you need more than a sales total. You need a year of books that adds up. Here is a practical checklist for **April 2025 to March 2026**:

1. **Every sale recorded.** Cash, card, QR and bank transfers, plus credit sales still unpaid at 31 March.
2. **Every purchase and expense, with its bill or receipt.** Gather supplier bills, utility bills, rent and transport. Anything without a document is hard to support later.
3. **Stock counted at 31 March 2026.** If you hold goods, your profit depends on what was left on the shelf at year end, not just what you bought.
4. **Assets bought during the year listed separately.** Vehicles, machinery, computers and fittings aren't ordinary expenses. Record each with its date and cost. Your accountant will work out the tax treatment.
5. **Loans kept apart from income.** A bank loan received isn't a sale, and loan repayments aren't all expenses. Record them on their own lines.
6. **Owner drawings separated from business costs.** Money you took out for household spending is a drawing, not a business expense.
7. **Bank statements reconciled to your books.** For every account, the closing balance at 31 March 2026 should match your records, and any difference should be explained.
8. **Partners: agree the shares.** A partnership return is due on the same date, and each partner declares their share. Agree the figures with your partners early.

## Why the exercise book struggles here

An exercise book or spreadsheet can work well day to day. The problems usually show up at year end, all at once:

- **Missing receipts** for costs you know you paid.
- **Personal and business spending mixed** in one account or one column.
- **No stock count** at 31 March, so profit is a guess.
- **Totals that don't match the bank**, with no easy way to see why.

None of these is hard to fix on its own. Fixing all of them in the last week of November, while your accountant is busy with every other client, is what turns a short job into a long one.
