Since **1 October 2026**, every VAT-registered business in Sri Lanka has had to issue tax invoices in the revised format set out in **Gazette Extraordinary No. 2481/22** (27 March 2026). If you still raise invoices from an Excel or Word template you set up years ago, now is a good time to check it against the new rules.

Here is a short checklist to run against your last few invoices.

## What changed, and when

- **27 March 2026:** Gazette 2481/22 set the revised format and specifications for VAT tax invoices. The IRD explained the rules in **Circular SEC/2026/E/03** (20 May 2026), which says the format is "legally binding" for all VAT-registered persons issuing invoices for taxable supplies. It also withdrew the earlier format under Gazette 2463/05.
- **6 August 2026:** Gazette 2500/106 moved the mandatory date from 1 July to **1 October 2026**. According to KPMG Sri Lanka, "all other matters" in Gazette 2481/22 stayed the same.

The rules were delayed once and that date has now passed, so treat them as in force today.

## The 2481/22 checklist

Take one of your recent tax invoices and go through these points. The source for each is the IRD circular.

**1. The words "TAX INVOICE"**
They must appear prominently, so the document can't be mistaken for a quotation, proforma or receipt.

**2. Your details, exactly as on your VAT registration certificate**

- Your 9-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)
- Your registered business name
- Your registered address

**3. Your buyer's details, when the buyer is VAT-registered**
Show the purchaser's TIN, name and address as they appear on _their_ VAT registration certificate. The IRD says this supports their input VAT claim.

**4. A serial number in the format `YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXXX`**

- **YY:** the first two digits of the year of issue (for example 26).
- **MMM:** the first three characters of the month (for example JUL).
- **QQQQ:** your own classification code, 1 to 15 letters, numbers or both. Use it for a branch, department, project, customer or invoice type.
- **XXXXX:** a running number made of **digits only**, with no letters or symbols. It should normally carry on from your last invoice. It may restart at a new month or year only where the reason is beyond your control.
- The full number must have **no spaces** and be **no more than 40 characters**.

**5. Two dates, not one**

- **Invoice date:** when the invoice was issued.
- **Date of supply:** when ownership of the goods or services passed.

This decides the correct VAT period. Many older templates have only one date box.

**6. A real description and a quantity**
Name what you actually supplied. The circular says to avoid vague words such as "items", "products", "services" or "miscellaneous". Give a quantity in a sensible unit: pieces, kilograms, litres, hours, days, cartons and so on.

**7. Amounts in rupees, broken down**
All amounts go in LKR with two decimal places, and each invoice must show:

- the value of supply excluding VAT;
- the VAT charged;
- the total including VAT.

If you invoice in foreign currency with Central Bank approval, you still have to show these three amounts converted into LKR as well.

**8. Only VAT-taxable supplies**
Exempt or out-of-scope supplies don't belong on a tax invoice. The exception: where they are directly related to, or an integral part of, the taxable supply, they may be shown separately.

**What you can still design yourself:** the circular lets you use your own layout, logo and extra columns, and treats payment mode, place of supply and amount in words as optional. What matters is that every mandatory item is there and easy to find.

### The narrow exception on serial numbers

You can leave out the `YYMMM_QQQQ_` part of the serial number only in one case. You must have obtained the Commissioner-General's approval **before 1 July 2026** to integrate your ERP with RAMIS through a Web API for real-time invoice transmission, and you must finish that integration **by 31 December 2026**. Most SMEs won't qualify, so plan on using the full format.

## Why your buyers will check your invoices

The IRD lists what can happen if an invoice doesn't follow the format. One of those things is "rejection of invoices for VAT input credit purposes". That risk falls on **your customer**, not just on you. If they are VAT-registered, a defective invoice from you could cost them input credit. Expect their accounts teams to start sending invoices back.

There is a legal side too. An IRD notice on the VAT (Amendment) Act No. 14 of 2026 says that, from 1 July 2026, failing to furnish valid tax invoices as required under section 21 is an offence under section 67 of the VAT Act. For offences committed on or after 1 October 2025, a person convicted can be fined up to **Rs. 1,000,000**.

E-invoicing is also moving forward: the IRD has published guidelines for sending tax invoices from business systems straight to RAMIS through a Web API, with a phased rollout. As more invoice data reaches the IRD automatically, invoices need to be right when issued.

## Why Excel and Word templates are the weak spot

As an invoicing system, spreadsheets and Word documents have three common problems:

- **Templates drift.** Someone saves a copy, deletes the date-of-supply box, or keeps last year's address.
- **Serial numbers get duplicated or skipped.** If two people copy the same file, the running number breaks. The IRD rule is that the sequence must stay continuous.
- **Nothing ties the invoice to your books.** The invoice says one thing and the VAT return is put together separately, so mistakes only show up later.

The fix is issuing invoices from the system that keeps your ledger.
