Tomorrow's SSCL return is the first one under a lower registration threshold. Many owners aren't sure it applies to them — June's headlines were confusing. Here is the rule, plainly, and a five-minute way to check where you stand.

## The rule, plainly

The **Social Security Contribution Levy (Amendment) Act, No. 10 of 2026**, certified on 9 April 2026, lowered the SSCL registration threshold for any period starting on or after **1 July 2026**:

|                                         | Before 1 July 2026 | From 1 July 2026   |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| Turnover in a quarter                   | Rs. 15 million     | **Rs. 9 million**  |
| Turnover over four consecutive quarters | Rs. 60 million     | **Rs. 36 million** |

The IRD's notice to taxpayers (PN/SSCL/2026-04/1, 16 April 2026) explains what this means in practice. If your aggregate turnover for July–September 2026, or any later quarter, exceeds or is likely to exceed Rs. 9 million, you must apply to register for SSCL **within 15 days** of that date. You then pay SSCL on your liable turnover from that quarter onwards.

The Act also updates the other end. For periods from 1 July 2026, the deregistration test is aggregate turnover over four consecutive quarters that doesn't exceed Rs. 36 million.

The VAT threshold **did not change**. In its notice on the VAT (Amendment) Act No. 14 of 2026, the IRD confirms that the proposed cut was abandoned. You still have to register for VAT when taxable supplies exceed, or are likely to exceed, **Rs. 15 million in a quarter** or **Rs. 60 million in the twelve months then ending**.

## Why so many people are unsure

A Budget proposal would have cut both thresholds to Rs. 36 million a year. On 23 June the Deputy Minister of Economic Development told Parliament that the cut would not go ahead, and some reports at the time said SSCL had stayed at Rs. 60 million as well.

It hadn't. In early July, Finance Ministry Tax Policy Adviser Thanuja Perera explained to the Daily FT that Parliament kept the Rs. 60 million threshold for **VAT**. The SSCL threshold, she said, had already been cut to Rs. 36 million under separate legislation and took effect on 1 July. Reports that SSCL had also gone back to Rs. 60 million "were incorrect".

Two laws, two paths: VAT stayed, SSCL came down.

## The new middle group: SSCL yes, VAT no

Because the two thresholds now differ, some businesses are inside SSCL but still outside VAT. Two examples, using only the thresholds:

- **A trader whose July–September turnover was Rs. 11 million.** That is above Rs. 9 million, so SSCL registration applies. It is below Rs. 15 million, so the quarterly VAT test isn't met.
- **A services firm with Rs. 48 million over the last four quarters.** That is above Rs. 36 million for SSCL and below Rs. 60 million for VAT.

Broadly, that's a business turning over Rs. 36–60 million a year.

**Two caveats before you decide you're in this group:**

- The IRD notice says the SSCL turnover threshold applies to taxable persons **other than a person who imports any article**. If you import, check your position with the IRD or your accountant.
- The IRD's VAT page says anyone **importing or exporting goods for commercial purposes** must register for VAT whatever their turnover. For them, "VAT no" may not apply at all.

## If you've crossed the threshold

1. **Apply to register for SSCL within 15 days** of the date your turnover exceeded, or became likely to exceed, the threshold.
2. **Pay SSCL from that quarter onwards**, and file the quarterly return.
3. **If you're unsure,** call the IRD hotline on 1944 or read notice PN/SSCL/2026-04/1 (linked below). Your accountant can confirm which of your sales count towards aggregate turnover.

The IRD's reminder notices say penalties apply to late or missed payments and returns; they don't give amounts, so we haven't either.

## Why this is really a bookkeeping question

You can only know whether you've crossed Rs. 9 million in a quarter, or Rs. 36 million over four consecutive quarters, if every sale is recorded completely and on time.

That's where exercise books and spreadsheets struggle. The four-quarter test rolls forward every quarter, late or missing entries throw the total off, and invoicing from one file while keeping accounts in another means two versions of the truth.

## Check where you stand: a five-minute checklist

1. Add up your turnover for the **last quarter** (July–September 2026).
2. Add up your turnover for the **last four consecutive quarters**.
3. Compare with the **SSCL** thresholds: Rs. 9 million a quarter, Rs. 36 million over four quarters.
4. Compare with the **VAT** thresholds: Rs. 15 million a quarter, Rs. 60 million over twelve months.
5. If you're over an SSCL threshold and not registered, **apply within 15 days**. Then put a reminder in your diary to run the check again when each quarter closes. The next one closes on 31 December.
