The IRD's move to real-time invoice reporting through RAMIS isn't a single switch thrown for every VAT-registered business. It's a phased rollout, and **Phase One is aimed squarely at export-oriented sectors** — tea and apparel/garment exporters being the first named groups, under **IRD Notice SEC/PN/VAT/2026-03** and subsequent phase notices.

If you export, this is the post for you. If you don't, the general [RAMIS e-invoicing explainer](/blog/ramis-e-invoicing-explained) is the better starting point — later phases will reach domestic sectors, but not yet.

## Why exporters first

It's a deliberate choice, and understanding the logic helps you plan.

Export transactions are **higher-value, lower-volume, and better-documented** than typical domestic retail. An apparel exporter issues a manageable number of large invoices against letters of credit and shipping documents, not thousands of small cash-register receipts. That makes the sector a clean place to prove the transmission pipeline. Export sectors also carry **zero-rated VAT** on the export leg, which means large, regular input-tax refund claims — exactly the area where the IRD gets the most value from seeing invoice data in real time rather than at return time.

So if you're a tea or garment exporter, you're not being singled out for scrutiny — you're in the group the IRD picked because your invoicing is the most structured to begin with.

## What being "in scope" requires of you

Concretely, three things:

- **A structured record of every tax invoice**, with issuer TIN, customer TIN, a separate [supply date](/blog/gazette-2481-22-explained), and line-level tax detail — the same fields Gazette 2481/22 already requires on the printed invoice, now also transmitted.
- **Invoices that are final when issued.** Real-time transmission and after-the-fact edits can't coexist. Any correction has to be a separate, dated credit note that is itself reported.
- **Handling for the acknowledgement.** When you transmit, RAMIS responds — accepted, or rejected with a reason. You need a place where a rejected invoice surfaces so someone can act on it, not a silent failure.

## The case for adopting before the mandate reaches you

Even if your exact phase date isn't confirmed, moving early is the low-risk choice, for reasons that have nothing to do with the IRD:

**The data problem doesn't wait.** If your invoicing today is a template in Word plus a spreadsheet, then the day transmission becomes mandatory you have two projects at once: restructure your invoice data _and_ wire up transmission, under a deadline. Adopting a system that already stores invoices in the right shape collapses that to one small step later.

**Refund claims get faster, not slower.** Exporters live on input-tax refunds. When the IRD can see your zero-rated export invoices as they're issued, the paper-trail questions that slow a refund down have already been answered. Being an early, clean transmitter is a cash-flow advantage.

**You find your data-quality problems on your schedule.** Missing customer TINs, inconsistent supply dates, credit notes that were really just edited invoices — every exporter has some of this. Better to surface it now, in a dry run, than have RAMIS reject a live invoice during a shipment week.
