The manufacturing module does the accounting for you — but it costs a build using the numbers already in your books. Get those right and your finished-goods valuation is defensible to the cent. Here's how to keep it that way.

## 1. Cost your raw materials before you build

A production order values each component at its current weighted-average cost, which comes from the [supplier bills](/blog/double-entry-for-sri-lankan-smes) you recorded when you bought it. If a material has never been billed and has no purchase price set, it costs zero — and your finished good is undervalued. Record the bill first. If you're migrating with stock already on hand, bring it in with an opening stock adjustment at a real cost.

## 2. Set wastage from what actually happens

The wastage field isn't a fudge factor — it's how much of a component a normal run consumes beyond the nominal quantity, because of offcuts, spillage, or spoilage. Measure it once from a real batch and enter it. A recipe that ignores 4% fabric wastage will quietly overstate your gross margin on every shirt.

## 3. Treat the recipe as a standard, and edit the order for exceptions

A bill of materials is a template. When one run uses a bit more of something, change it on the **production order**, not the recipe — the "Reset to recipe" button is right there. That keeps your standard recipe meaningful and your run-to-run variances visible.

## 4. Read the posting preview every time

Before you issue, the order shows you the journal entry it will post. Thirty seconds checking that the debit lands on your finished-goods account and the credits come off the right raw-materials accounts will catch a mis-mapped item long before it's buried in a month of transactions.

## 5. Decide whether to split your inventory accounts

You can run everything through one Inventory account, or map raw materials to a **Raw Materials Inventory** account and finished goods to a **Finished Goods Inventory** account. The split makes your balance sheet show work-in-progress value moving through the business, and the valuation report reconciles across all of them. It's a one-time choice per item.

## 6. Void, never delete

Got a build wrong? Void the issued order. It posts the exact reverse and restores every item's stock — a build and its reversal, both on the record. A production order you can quietly delete is one an auditor can't trust.

---

None of this is extra bookkeeping. It's the same discipline the rest of Ledgerly asks for — every number traced to a document — applied to the point where your materials become product.
